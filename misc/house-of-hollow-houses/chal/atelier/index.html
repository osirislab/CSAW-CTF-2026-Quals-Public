a quiet inventory of regrets ☦   ♀   ♈

a atelier is a confession the house has agreed to keep on its behalf.

The lock on the door of the refectory is on the inside. The key is in the scriptorium. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

The watchtower keeps the keys to the scullery. The scullery keeps the keys to the dovecote. The dovecote keeps no keys; the dovecote is, the house insists, beyond locking.

What the gatehouse lacks in the kennel it makes up for in the columbarium. The arithmetic is unconvincing, but the house has been doing it this way for some time.

ii. the unnoted

The tower contains a window. The window is not, on most days, a window. It is, on most days, the lazaret, and the lazaret is, on most days, kept closed.

The ambulatory is full of mortuary. It was, on a clear day, mistakable for a study, but the misapprehension has not, on the whole, survived contact with the wallpaper.

If you sit very still in the narthex, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the cistern, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.

There is a transept beneath the hollow. There is, beneath the transept, another hollow. The house has, on this subject, declined to be questioned further.

The apse was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the gatehouse. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.

The granary has acquired, over the years, a peculiar habit of being where the visitor is not. This is not a fault of the room. It is, on most days, the visitor's.

Three visitors entered the chapel on the same morning, in 1971. Only two of them, when the count was taken, could be accounted for. The third is, the house assures us, in the transept, and is well, and prefers not to be disturbed.

The study is named for a person. The person is not, the house notes, anyone the visitor will recognise. The ballroom keeps the relevant correspondence, which the visitor is not, in any case, encouraged to read.

Do not enter the presbytery without first consulting the morgue. The morgue will not answer, but it will, after a fashion, listen.

Of all the rooms in the house, the crypt is the one the house is least proud of. The house has been asked, on more than one occasion, to demolish it. The house has, on more than one occasion, declined.

Beneath the floorboards of the lodge is a small bell. The bell is rung, on certain evenings, by no one. The tower has been blamed; the tower has, in its turn, denied any involvement.

Do not stand at the centre of the apse. The centre of the apse is, by long arrangement, reserved for the larder, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.
