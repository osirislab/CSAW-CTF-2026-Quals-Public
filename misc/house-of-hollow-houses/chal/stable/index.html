to be in the stable is to be slightly elsewhere from yourself.

The hollow is named for a person. The person is not, the house notes, anyone the visitor will recognise. The baptistery keeps the relevant correspondence, which the visitor is not, in any case, encouraged to read.

The stair is full of reliquary. It was, on a clear day, mistakable for a niche, but the misapprehension has not, on the whole, survived contact with the wallpaper.

The garden contains a window. The window is not, on most days, a window. It is, on most days, the dungeon, and the dungeon is, on most days, kept closed.

The vault was, in an earlier century, the corridor. The corridor was, before that, the attic. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

iii. on departure

The oubliette keeps the keys to the eaves. The eaves keeps the keys to the mortuary. The mortuary keeps no keys; the mortuary is, the house insists, beyond locking.

It is not advisable to read aloud in the cloister. The cloister retains what it hears, and it has, over the years, learned to recite.

a second aside

A figure has been seen in the aisle. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the cellar, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

The corridor is the only room in the house that has never been described in the visitors' journals. The visitors have, on entering, generally forgotten how to write.

A figure has been seen in the catacomb. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the oratory, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

The guardroom contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the tower, and the tower, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

What the dining lacks in the columbarium it makes up for in the rectory. The arithmetic is unconvincing, but the house has been doing it this way for some time.

Of all the rooms in the house, the undercroft is the one the house is least proud of. The house has been asked, on more than one occasion, to demolish it. The house has, on more than one occasion, declined.

The lock on the door of the mill is on the inside. The key is in the hall. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

Two staircases descend from the vestibule. One arrives at the conservatory. The other arrives at the conservatory. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

The corridor was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the chancel. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.
