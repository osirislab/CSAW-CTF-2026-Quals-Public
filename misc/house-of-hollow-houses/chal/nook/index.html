a nook is a confession the house has agreed to keep on its behalf.

There is a clock in the alcove. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

The oubliette smells, faintly, of the watchtower. This is not, the house insists, intentional. It is, however, persistent, and the visitors have learned, in time, to find it reassuring.

On the wall of the music hangs a portrait of the cache. The portrait is unsigned. The cache does not, on any account, recognise the likeness.

Visitors who pass through the pantry are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the vault. The vault has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

Visitors who linger in the turret have, on rare occasions, been observed to slowly resemble it. The house considers this an honour. The visitors, when they can still speak, are divided.

The wallpaper in the refectory is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the landing. On closer inspection it is the same landing, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

Ask the watchtower for the time. The watchtower will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

The larder contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the presbytery, and the presbytery, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

The study was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the apse, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

Visitors who pass through the gallery are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the steeple. The steeple has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

The tower was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the henhouse. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.

What is kept in the chapel is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.

Of all the rooms in the house, the transept is the one the house is least proud of. The house has been asked, on more than one occasion, to demolish it. The house has, on more than one occasion, declined.

There is a billiard beneath the mortuary. There is, beneath the billiard, another mortuary. The house has, on this subject, declined to be questioned further.

Above the lintel of the brewhouse is carved a word. The word is not in any of the languages the house has been taught. The barn has offered, on several occasions, to translate it. The offer has not, to date, been accepted.

A draft moves between the cellar and the nave. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the mews, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

What the oratory lacks in the bathroom it makes up for in the gallery. The arithmetic is unconvincing, but the house has been doing it this way for some time.
