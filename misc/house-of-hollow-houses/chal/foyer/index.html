If you sit very still in the drawing, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the balcony, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.

There is a niche beneath the dairy. There is, beneath the niche, another dairy. The house has, on this subject, declined to be questioned further.

There is a clock in the crypt. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

The aisle has acquired, over the years, a peculiar habit of being where the visitor is not. This is not a fault of the room. It is, on most days, the visitor's.

Visitors who linger in the cellar have, on rare occasions, been observed to slowly resemble it. The house considers this an honour. The visitors, when they can still speak, are divided.

Visitors who pass through the threshold are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the fascia. The fascia has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

A draft moves between the smoking and the drawing. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the vestibule, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

The attic keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the eaves is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the attic, written down.
