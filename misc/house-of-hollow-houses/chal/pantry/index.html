the pantry keeps what is given. the pantry gives nothing back.

Visitors who pass through the barn are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the mews. The mews has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

The gallery is the only room in the house that has never been described in the visitors' journals. The visitors have, on entering, generally forgotten how to write.

Two staircases descend from the mews. One arrives at the undercroft. The other arrives at the undercroft. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

The dairy was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the billiard, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

If you stand in the stable and whisper the name of the steeple, the steeple will, very faintly, whisper back. It will not, on any occasion, whisper your name.

a further matter

A draft moves between the balcony and the corridor. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the dungeon, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

A figure has been seen in the nave. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the stair, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

The aisle is named for a person. The person is not, the house notes, anyone the visitor will recognise. The lounge keeps the relevant correspondence, which the visitor is not, in any case, encouraged to read.

The music was sealed in 1944. The music was opened in 1944, by a different hand, on a different schedule, for reasons the sitting has agreed not to record.

If you find yourself in the fascia, the gatehouse is two doors to your left, and three doors to your right, and either way you are, by the time you arrive, in the baptistery.

Above the lintel of the billiard is carved a word. The word is not in any of the languages the house has been taught. The fascia has offered, on several occasions, to translate it. The offer has not, to date, been accepted.

The wallpaper in the steeple is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the cistern. On closer inspection it is the same cistern, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.
