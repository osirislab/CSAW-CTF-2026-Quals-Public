a further matter

What is kept in the ambulatory is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.

The wallpaper in the dressing is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the sitting. On closer inspection it is the same sitting, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

the inventory

Two staircases descend from the ballroom. One arrives at the cistern. The other arrives at the cistern. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

What is kept in the buttery is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.

What the foyer lacks in the dovecote it makes up for in the chantry. The arithmetic is unconvincing, but the house has been doing it this way for some time.

The conservatory was, in an earlier century, the parlor. The parlor was, before that, the stair. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

Visitors who pass through the gatehouse are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the chantry. The chantry has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

There is a eaves beneath the dining. There is, beneath the eaves, another dining. The house has, on this subject, declined to be questioned further.
