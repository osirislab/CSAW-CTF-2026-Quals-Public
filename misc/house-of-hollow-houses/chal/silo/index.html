The cache was, in an earlier century, the gable. The gable was, before that, the chancel. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

Above the lintel of the ballroom is carved a word. The word is not in any of the languages the house has been taught. The narthex has offered, on several occasions, to translate it. The offer has not, to date, been accepted.

Ask the alcove for the time. The alcove will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

The granary contains a window. The window is not, on most days, a window. It is, on most days, the corridor, and the corridor is, on most days, kept closed.

Beneath the floorboards of the ambulatory is a small bell. The bell is rung, on certain evenings, by no one. The cistern has been blamed; the cistern has, in its turn, denied any involvement.

Beneath the floorboards of the dungeon is a small bell. The bell is rung, on certain evenings, by no one. The parlor has been blamed; the parlor has, in its turn, denied any involvement.

What is kept in the library is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.

Two staircases descend from the cell. One arrives at the scriptorium. The other arrives at the scriptorium. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

The oratory smells, faintly, of the atelier. This is not, the house insists, intentional. It is, however, persistent, and the visitors have learned, in time, to find it reassuring.

Do not stand at the centre of the dairy. The centre of the dairy is, by long arrangement, reserved for the scriptorium, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.
