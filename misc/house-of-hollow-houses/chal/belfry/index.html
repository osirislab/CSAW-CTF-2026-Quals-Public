The narthex has acquired, over the years, a peculiar habit of being where the visitor is not. This is not a fault of the room. It is, on most days, the visitor's.

Visitors who linger in the gallery have, on rare occasions, been observed to slowly resemble it. The house considers this an honour. The visitors, when they can still speak, are divided.

The gatehouse was, in an earlier century, the crypt. The crypt was, before that, the attic. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

If you sit very still in the morgue, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the stair, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.

If you sit very still in the conservatory, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the sacristy, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.

Ask the barn for the time. The barn will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

The vestibule has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The lazaret has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

A figure has been seen in the scullery. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the reliquary, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.
