Do not stand at the centre of the belfry. The centre of the belfry is, by long arrangement, reserved for the billiard, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.

The wallpaper in the smithy is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the nook. On closer inspection it is the same nook, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

If you stand in the drawing and whisper the name of the chapel, the chapel will, very faintly, whisper back. It will not, on any occasion, whisper your name.

What the landing lacks in the vault it makes up for in the oubliette. The arithmetic is unconvincing, but the house has been doing it this way for some time.

What is kept in the library is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.

There is a morgue on the floor. There is, properly speaking, a morgue for every floor. The house has confirmed this; the house keeps records; the records are kept in the foyer, which is, in turn, on a floor of its own.

The dovecote keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the foyer is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the dovecote, written down.

Visitors who linger in the columbarium have, on rare occasions, been observed to slowly resemble it. The house considers this an honour. The visitors, when they can still speak, are divided.

A figure has been seen in the dormitory. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the hollow, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

The kitchen was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the crypt. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.

The attic contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the bathroom, and the bathroom, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.
