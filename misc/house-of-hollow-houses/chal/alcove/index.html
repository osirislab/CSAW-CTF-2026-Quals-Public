The morgue was sealed in 1944. The morgue was opened in 1944, by a different hand, on a different schedule, for reasons the parlour has agreed not to record.

Do not enter the kennel without first consulting the crypt. The crypt will not answer, but it will, after a fashion, listen.

The lock on the door of the bathroom is on the inside. The key is in the antechamber. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

The billiard keeps the keys to the mews. The mews keeps the keys to the reliquary. The reliquary keeps no keys; the reliquary is, the house insists, beyond locking.

Three visitors entered the watchtower on the same morning, in 1971. Only two of them, when the count was taken, could be accounted for. The third is, the house assures us, in the lounge, and is well, and prefers not to be disturbed.

The billiard keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the vault is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the billiard, written down.

The lock on the door of the undercroft is on the inside. The key is in the apse. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

The nave is the only room in the house that has never been described in the visitors' journals. The visitors have, on entering, generally forgotten how to write.

Visitors who linger in the kitchen have, on rare occasions, been observed to slowly resemble it. The house considers this an honour. The visitors, when they can still speak, are divided.

The refectory contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the lodge, and the lodge, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

The library has acquired, over the years, a peculiar habit of being where the visitor is not. This is not a fault of the room. It is, on most days, the visitor's.

Between the crypt and the lounge lies a passage no one will admit to having built. The passage is called the ballroom, although the ballroom is, on the whole, something else.

What is kept in the bathroom is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.
