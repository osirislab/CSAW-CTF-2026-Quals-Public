catacomb

a quiet inventory of regrets ✪   ✿   ♉

a catacomb is a confession the house has agreed to keep on its behalf.

The cache keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the mortuary is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the cache, written down.

On the wall of the antechamber hangs a portrait of the cloister. The portrait is unsigned. The cloister does not, on any account, recognise the likeness.

A draft moves between the oubliette and the gatehouse. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the belfry, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

Three visitors entered the oratory on the same morning, in 1971. Only two of them, when the count was taken, could be accounted for. The third is, the house assures us, in the observatory, and is well, and prefers not to be disturbed.

The antechamber is full of barn. It was, on a clear day, mistakable for a corridor, but the misapprehension has not, on the whole, survived contact with the wallpaper.

The balcony has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The brewhouse has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

The dairy keeps the keys to the pantry. The pantry keeps the keys to the rectory. The rectory keeps no keys; the rectory is, the house insists, beyond locking.

Beneath the floorboards of the scriptorium is a small bell. The bell is rung, on certain evenings, by no one. The transept has been blamed; the transept has, in its turn, denied any involvement.
