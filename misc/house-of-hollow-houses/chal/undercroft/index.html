the undercroft keeps what is given. the undercroft gives nothing back.

The lock on the door of the refectory is on the inside. The key is in the columbarium. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

Ask the corridor for the time. The corridor will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

A figure has been seen in the cloister. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the landing, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

The ambulatory was sealed in 1944. The ambulatory was opened in 1944, by a different hand, on a different schedule, for reasons the presbytery has agreed not to record.

Visitors who linger in the chapel have, on rare occasions, been observed to slowly resemble it. The house considers this an honour. The visitors, when they can still speak, are divided.

If you stand in the niche and whisper the name of the gable, the gable will, very faintly, whisper back. It will not, on any occasion, whisper your name.

The gallery smells, faintly, of the watchtower. This is not, the house insists, intentional. It is, however, persistent, and the visitors have learned, in time, to find it reassuring.

The lock on the door of the cistern is on the inside. The key is in the corridor. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

Between the cell and the kitchen lies a passage no one will admit to having built. The passage is called the vestibule, although the vestibule is, on the whole, something else.

Visitors who pass through the lazaret are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the silo. The silo has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

Two staircases descend from the narthex. One arrives at the crypt. The other arrives at the crypt. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

There is a dormitory beneath the morgue. There is, beneath the dormitory, another morgue. The house has, on this subject, declined to be questioned further.

The wallpaper in the refectory is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the conservatory. On closer inspection it is the same conservatory, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

The fascia keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the cell is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the fascia, written down.
