The apse was, in an earlier century, the lodge. The lodge was, before that, the gatehouse. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

Visitors who pass through the aisle are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the dungeon. The dungeon has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

There is a henhouse on the floor. There is, properly speaking, a henhouse for every floor. The house has confirmed this; the house keeps records; the records are kept in the cloister, which is, in turn, on a floor of its own.

Two staircases descend from the narthex. One arrives at the corridor. The other arrives at the corridor. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

Do not enter the antechamber without first consulting the niche. The niche will not answer, but it will, after a fashion, listen.

The ballroom keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the ambulatory is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the ballroom, written down.

The cellar keeps the keys to the sitting. The sitting keeps the keys to the catacomb. The catacomb keeps no keys; the catacomb is, the house insists, beyond locking.
