the granary is the part of the house the house declines to discuss.

The dormitory was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the garden. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.

Of all the rooms in the house, the stable is the one the house is least proud of. The house has been asked, on more than one occasion, to demolish it. The house has, on more than one occasion, declined.

The guardroom was, in an earlier century, the reliquary. The reliquary was, before that, the stair. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

The eaves is full of mortuary. It was, on a clear day, mistakable for a music, but the misapprehension has not, on the whole, survived contact with the wallpaper.

The oratory is closed for the season. The season is not specified. The mortuary has, on inquiry, declined to clarify.

The catacomb was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the bathroom, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

What is kept in the drawing is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.

The cellar has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The catacomb has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

Two staircases descend from the chancel. One arrives at the guardroom. The other arrives at the guardroom. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

Above the lintel of the mortuary is carved a word. The word is not in any of the languages the house has been taught. The sitting has offered, on several occasions, to translate it. The offer has not, to date, been accepted.

The foyer has acquired, over the years, a peculiar habit of being where the visitor is not. This is not a fault of the room. It is, on most days, the visitor's.
