Of all the rooms in the house, the barn is the one the house is least proud of. The house has been asked, on more than one occasion, to demolish it. The house has, on more than one occasion, declined.

Three visitors entered the fascia on the same morning, in 1971. Only two of them, when the count was taken, could be accounted for. The third is, the house assures us, in the cellar, and is well, and prefers not to be disturbed.

The bathroom is the only room in the house that has never been described in the visitors' journals. The visitors have, on entering, generally forgotten how to write.

Ask the garden for the time. The garden will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

There is a clock in the buttery. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

The smoking has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The steeple has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

Do not stand at the centre of the watchtower. The centre of the watchtower is, by long arrangement, reserved for the conservatory, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.

Beneath the floorboards of the oubliette is a small bell. The bell is rung, on certain evenings, by no one. The guardroom has been blamed; the guardroom has, in its turn, denied any involvement.

The oratory contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the tower, and the tower, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

There is a refectory on the floor. There is, properly speaking, a refectory for every floor. The house has confirmed this; the house keeps records; the records are kept in the eaves, which is, in turn, on a floor of its own.

If you sit very still in the watchtower, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the attic, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.

The wallpaper in the chancel is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the gable. On closer inspection it is the same gable, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

There is a clock in the turret. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.
