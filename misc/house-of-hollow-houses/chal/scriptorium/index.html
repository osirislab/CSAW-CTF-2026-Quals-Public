a scriptorium is a room which has been asked to apologise for itself.

There is a transept on the floor. There is, properly speaking, a transept for every floor. The house has confirmed this; the house keeps records; the records are kept in the attic, which is, in turn, on a floor of its own.

The atelier was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the ballroom, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

It is not advisable to read aloud in the chancel. The chancel retains what it hears, and it has, over the years, learned to recite.

On the wall of the apse hangs a portrait of the cistern. The portrait is unsigned. The cistern does not, on any account, recognise the likeness.

The mill was sealed in 1944. The mill was opened in 1944, by a different hand, on a different schedule, for reasons the vestibule has agreed not to record.

Ask the pantry for the time. The pantry will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

The drawing contains a window. The window is not, on most days, a window. It is, on most days, the refectory, and the refectory is, on most days, kept closed.

There is a foyer on the floor. There is, properly speaking, a foyer for every floor. The house has confirmed this; the house keeps records; the records are kept in the smithy, which is, in turn, on a floor of its own.

Two staircases descend from the vault. One arrives at the stable. The other arrives at the stable. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

The aisle is the only room in the house that has never been described in the visitors' journals. The visitors have, on entering, generally forgotten how to write.

The gallery keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the presbytery is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the gallery, written down.
