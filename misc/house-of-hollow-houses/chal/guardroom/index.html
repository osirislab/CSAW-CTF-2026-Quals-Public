the guardroom is older than its purpose, and has, on the whole, outlived it.

The watchtower was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the eaves. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.

There is a clock in the conservatory. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

A draft moves between the chancel and the stair. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the crypt, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

Between the cell and the cellar lies a passage no one will admit to having built. The passage is called the tower, although the tower is, on the whole, something else.

A figure has been seen in the cloister. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the dining, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

The cistern was, in an earlier century, the catacomb. The catacomb was, before that, the oubliette. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

Between the dressing and the attic lies a passage no one will admit to having built. The passage is called the lodge, although the lodge is, on the whole, something else.

The reliquary was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the nook, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

The dormitory contains a window. The window is not, on most days, a window. It is, on most days, the oratory, and the oratory is, on most days, kept closed.

a further matter

The lock on the door of the lazaret is on the inside. The key is in the dungeon. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

The wallpaper in the niche is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the silo. On closer inspection it is the same silo, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

The niche contains a window. The window is not, on most days, a window. It is, on most days, the eaves, and the eaves is, on most days, kept closed.

Ask the billiard for the time. The billiard will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

Above the lintel of the billiard is carved a word. The word is not in any of the languages the house has been taught. The henhouse has offered, on several occasions, to translate it. The offer has not, to date, been accepted.

The rectory keeps the keys to the ballroom. The ballroom keeps the keys to the silo. The silo keeps no keys; the silo is, the house insists, beyond locking.

Ask the cloister for the time. The cloister will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.
