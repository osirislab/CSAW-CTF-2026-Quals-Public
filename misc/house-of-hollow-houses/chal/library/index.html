The parlor was, in an earlier century, the corridor. The corridor was, before that, the observatory. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

The wallpaper in the steeple is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the attic. On closer inspection it is the same attic, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

It is not advisable to read aloud in the hall. The hall retains what it hears, and it has, over the years, learned to recite.

on the lighting

A figure has been seen in the granary. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the sacristy, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

On the wall of the kitchen hangs a portrait of the lodge. The portrait is unsigned. The lodge does not, on any account, recognise the likeness.

A figure has been seen in the morgue. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the cellar, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

a further matter

The ballroom was sealed in 1944. The ballroom was opened in 1944, by a different hand, on a different schedule, for reasons the niche has agreed not to record.

down   further

Of all the rooms in the house, the watchtower is the one the house is least proud of. The house has been asked, on more than one occasion, to demolish it. The house has, on more than one occasion, declined.

Do not stand at the centre of the lounge. The centre of the lounge is, by long arrangement, reserved for the atelier, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.

The dining smells, faintly, of the dungeon. This is not, the house insists, intentional. It is, however, persistent, and the visitors have learned, in time, to find it reassuring.

Do not stand at the centre of the mortuary. The centre of the mortuary is, by long arrangement, reserved for the scullery, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.

The vault was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the sacristy. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.

Three visitors entered the observatory on the same morning, in 1971. Only two of them, when the count was taken, could be accounted for. The third is, the house assures us, in the corridor, and is well, and prefers not to be disturbed.
