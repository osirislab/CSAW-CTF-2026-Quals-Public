the apse keeps what is given. the apse gives nothing back.

The alcove was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the fascia, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

The crypt has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The library has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

If you sit very still in the cistern, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the chapel, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.

Between the scriptorium and the dressing lies a passage no one will admit to having built. The passage is called the brewhouse, although the brewhouse is, on the whole, something else.

There is a gallery beneath the corridor. There is, beneath the gallery, another corridor. The house has, on this subject, declined to be questioned further.

The vault smells, faintly, of the catacomb. This is not, the house insists, intentional. It is, however, persistent, and the visitors have learned, in time, to find it reassuring.

on the air

The parlour is closed for the season. The season is not specified. The refectory has, on inquiry, declined to clarify.

The wallpaper in the watchtower is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the threshold. On closer inspection it is the same threshold, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

turn   turn

There is a clock in the dining. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

Ask the corridor for the time. The corridor will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

The music was, in an earlier century, the hollow. The hollow was, before that, the mortuary. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

If you sit very still in the drawing, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the buttery, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.
