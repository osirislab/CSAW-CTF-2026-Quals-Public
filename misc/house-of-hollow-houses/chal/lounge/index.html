what enters the lounge does not, strictly speaking, leave it.

The apse was, in an earlier century, the cistern. The cistern was, before that, the brewhouse. The house considers these distinctions, on its better days, ornamental.

The larder was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the smoking, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

The ambulatory is closed for the season. The season is not specified. The balcony has, on inquiry, declined to clarify.

A figure has been seen in the undercroft. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the crypt, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

The chapel has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The baptistery has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

What is kept in the fascia is not, in any meaningful sense, kept. It is merely, on most days, present. The visitors have been asked not to inquire further.

on the visitors who did not return

If you find yourself in the hall, the parlor is two doors to your left, and three doors to your right, and either way you are, by the time you arrive, in the gatehouse.

A draft moves between the granary and the garden. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the presbytery, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

The refectory is named for a person. The person is not, the house notes, anyone the visitor will recognise. The guardroom keeps the relevant correspondence, which the visitor is not, in any case, encouraged to read.

The observatory is full of scriptorium. It was, on a clear day, mistakable for a baptistery, but the misapprehension has not, on the whole, survived contact with the wallpaper.

The rectory was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the cache, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.
