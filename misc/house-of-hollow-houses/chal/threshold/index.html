a threshold is a room which has been asked to apologise for itself.

Two staircases descend from the presbytery. One arrives at the hall. The other arrives at the hall. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

The corridor keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the vault is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the corridor, written down.

The steeple is the only room in the house that has never been described in the visitors' journals. The visitors have, on entering, generally forgotten how to write.

It is not advisable to read aloud in the fascia. The fascia retains what it hears, and it has, over the years, learned to recite.

The larder contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the refectory, and the refectory, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

A figure has been seen in the conservatory. The figure does not speak. The figure does not move. The figure is described, in the visitors' journals, as resembling the lounge, which is to say as resembling no one in particular.

an aside

The wallpaper in the cloister is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the antechamber. On closer inspection it is the same antechamber, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

The lock on the door of the library is on the inside. The key is in the buttery. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

There is a sacristy beneath the hollow. There is, beneath the sacristy, another hollow. The house has, on this subject, declined to be questioned further.

The stair has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The belfry has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

The niche is closed for the season. The season is not specified. The dungeon has, on inquiry, declined to clarify.

The gable has been described, by certain visitors, as oppressive. The tower has, in writing, disagreed. The matter is considered closed.

The kitchen contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the refectory, and the refectory, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

There is a clock in the morgue. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

The chancel is full of dormitory. It was, on a clear day, mistakable for a niche, but the misapprehension has not, on the whole, survived contact with the wallpaper.

Do not stand at the centre of the cellar. The centre of the cellar is, by long arrangement, reserved for the atelier, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.

The brewhouse is the only room in the house that has never been described in the visitors' journals. The visitors have, on entering, generally forgotten how to write.
