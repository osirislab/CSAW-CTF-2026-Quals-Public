Of all the rooms in the house, the oubliette is the one the house is least proud of. The house has been asked, on more than one occasion, to demolish it. The house has, on more than one occasion, declined.

Visitors who linger in the antechamber have, on rare occasions, been observed to slowly resemble it. The house considers this an honour. The visitors, when they can still speak, are divided.

a note from the caretaker

The wallpaper in the narthex is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the garden. On closer inspection it is the same garden, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

The mews contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the watchtower, and the watchtower, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

Visitors who pass through the catacomb are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the transept. The transept has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

what the room is not for

The barn was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the undercroft, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

There is a clock in the granary. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

The columbarium keeps the keys to the vault. The vault keeps the keys to the tower. The tower keeps no keys; the tower is, the house insists, beyond locking.
