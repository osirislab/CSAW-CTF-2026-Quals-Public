the dungeon is the part of the house the house declines to discuss.

Do not stand at the centre of the eaves. The centre of the eaves is, by long arrangement, reserved for the columbarium, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.

The fascia keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the dormitory is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the fascia, written down.

There is a clock in the balcony. The clock keeps perfect time. The time it keeps perfect is not, however, the time being kept anywhere else in the building.

The larder keeps the keys to the antechamber. The antechamber keeps the keys to the barn. The barn keeps no keys; the barn is, the house insists, beyond locking.

There is a landing on the floor. There is, properly speaking, a landing for every floor. The house has confirmed this; the house keeps records; the records are kept in the buttery, which is, in turn, on a floor of its own.

A draft moves between the smoking and the library. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the gable, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

The parlor was decorated, in 1903, by a woman who is now in the library. She has not, on any subsequent occasion, asked to be moved.

Do not stand at the centre of the foyer. The centre of the foyer is, by long arrangement, reserved for the lazaret, which is not, at the time of writing, in the building.

The gable keeps a list. The list is of every visitor who has ever asked, on entering, where the bathroom is. The list is bound; the list is long; the list is the gable, written down.

The threshold is closed for the season. The season is not specified. The cache has, on inquiry, declined to clarify.

The parlor was sealed in 1944. The parlor was opened in 1944, by a different hand, on a different schedule, for reasons the attic has agreed not to record.

The lock on the door of the cell is on the inside. The key is in the dressing. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

If you sit very still in the gallery, you may hear the floor above shift, slightly, in your direction. The floor above is the study, and is, at the time of writing, unoccupied.

Ask the landing for the time. The landing will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.
