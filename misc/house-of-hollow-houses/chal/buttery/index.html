the buttery is the part of the house the house declines to discuss.

The wallpaper in the dairy is, on inspection, a repeating pattern of the belfry. On closer inspection it is the same belfry, every time. On closer inspection still it is the visitor.

Ask the music for the time. The music will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

If you stand in the apse and whisper the name of the belfry, the belfry will, very faintly, whisper back. It will not, on any occasion, whisper your name.

Ask the larder for the time. The larder will tell you it is later than you think. It will not specify than what.

The scriptorium smells, faintly, of the fascia. This is not, the house insists, intentional. It is, however, persistent, and the visitors have learned, in time, to find it reassuring.

Visitors who pass through the library are expected to leave their hats at the door. The door is in the brewhouse. The brewhouse has, since 1947, kept the hats in a basket the colour of which no one has been able to agree on.

The kennel contains a chair. The chair contains an opinion. The opinion concerns the catacomb, and the catacomb, on hearing it, has elected to remain silent.

The granary was, at one point, twice its present size. The other half is in the drawing, and is not, the house insists, available for inspection.

The library contains a window. The window is not, on most days, a window. It is, on most days, the observatory, and the observatory is, on most days, kept closed.

The sitting has acquired, over the years, a peculiar habit of being where the visitor is not. This is not a fault of the room. It is, on most days, the visitor's.

A draft moves between the foyer and the mill. The draft, on cold nights, carries the sound of a conversation that took place in the dining, in a year the house has been asked not to specify.

The lock on the door of the balcony is on the inside. The key is in the foyer. Visitors are advised to plan accordingly.

The transept has acquired, over the years, a peculiar habit of being where the visitor is not. This is not a fault of the room. It is, on most days, the visitor's.

If you find yourself in the conservatory, the antechamber is two doors to your left, and three doors to your right, and either way you are, by the time you arrive, in the baptistery.

Two staircases descend from the crypt. One arrives at the kitchen. The other arrives at the kitchen. The arrival is, in either case, the same; the descent is not.

There is a ambulatory on the floor. There is, properly speaking, a ambulatory for every floor. The house has confirmed this; the house keeps records; the records are kept in the vestibule, which is, in turn, on a floor of its own.
